## What a hero is

- **A regiment of one.** A hero is a one-model regiment of its own unit type, with the roster's stats, that never routs. It moves, fights, takes orders and is selected like any regiment, so formations, brigades, gates, bridges and every movement rule already apply to it. Its unit is in the Conquest unit table with a hero flag, so the sandbox and Field Battles can place it too.
- **Twelve heroes, three a faction**, from the rosters: the Daimyo, Ronin and Onmyoji (Clans), the Queen-in-Waiting, Devourer and Whisperer (Brood), the Maestro, Refractor and Hollow Bell (Choir), the Magnate, Undertaker and Usurer (Concord). Each is hired at its faction's hero building (Ancestor Shrine, Queen's Chamber, Sanctum of Echoes, Vault of Names).
- **Hire:** 200 Grub and 50 Marrow, 40 s, 5 supply, as the rosters say. A player can have one hero at tier 1, two at tier 2, three at tier 3, counting the living, the dead and the one in training, and each named hero once.
- **Levels 1 to 10.** Experience is the full hit points of every soldier hostile to the hero's player who dies within 24 tiles of the hero, whatever killed him (blows, volleys, towers, fire, powers), shared evenly between that player's heroes in reach; allies' heroes share too if they are in reach. Heroes level by being where battles are, as in Warcraft III. Levels come at 200, 450, 750, 1100, 1500, 1950, 2450, 3000 and 3600 experience. Tier-1 regiments hold 128 to 252 hit points and rout at 35 to 45% strength, so only about 60% of an army's hit points die; level 2 is about one regiment's worth killed near the hero, level 10 about 25 regiments' worth, a long Conquest game for a hero with its army. The curve is tuned with the Conquest soak. Each level adds the roster's `per_level` hit points and damage, heals the new hit points (H1 banked a skill point a level; H2 dropped skill points). Heroes gain no veteran ranks: levels are their experience.
- **Abilities (stage H2, built):** two a hero, a basic ability with three ranks (from levels 1, 4 and 7) and an ultimate from level 6, with no skill points: they grow with the hero's level by themselves ([44 · Hero abilities](44-hero-abilities.md)). Items wait for creep camps and merchants, which aren't built.
- **Leadership.** The Field Battle general's rules, shared code: every regiment of the hero's player within 16 tiles is led, as the general leads: it gains 1 morale point a second (scaled by the battle's pace) and its breaking point is 5 points lower; the Concord's undead (by the units' undead flag, not by having no morale, so workers and heroes are not counted) strike 10% harder instead. Being led is one flag, so two heroes near a regiment lead it no more than one. Not while the hero is routing (it never routs) or in a cocoon. The rosters' "25% less from casualties" is dropped: the breaking point is what decides a rout.
- **When a hero falls,** every regiment of its player within 16 tiles loses 10 morale, as at a general's fall (a hero falls more often than a general, so its player's whole army doesn't panic as well), and undead within 16 tiles fall into disorder. Swarm Instinct's "50% more when a nearby hero dies" is not built yet (it is a follow-up).
- **Revive:** a dead hero is revived at any finished hero building of its owner, keeping its level, experience and ranks, for its hire price and time times 50% + 15% a level (level 1: 130 Grub, 32 Marrow, 26 s; level 10: 400 Grub, 100 Marrow, 80 s), the time scaled by the match's tempo like training. It uses no supply while dead. It comes out at the building like a trained regiment, at full hit points.
- **Champions:** a champion is a soldier of a regiment of two or more, so heroes are never picked in H1. In H3 a hero becomes its side's champion, and a hero who loses the duel is wounded rather than killed.

## Stages

- **H1, heroes in Conquest:** the hero table (baked from the rosters' `heroes`), hiring with the tier limit, levels and experience, per-level growth, leadership and the fall, revive; the hero panel fed with real data; computer players put up their hero building at tier 2 once they have an army, hire one hero as soon as they have it, the next at each tier, revive a fallen one when they can pay, and march it with the army; stress scenario `heroes`; Guide, Quick Start and building tips.
- **H2, abilities (built 2026-11-22):** cut down by Mario to two abilities a hero, 24 in all, with no skill points; the engine, `Command::Cast`, the command grid's `A` and `S`, computer players casting by simple rules, the `abilities` stress scenario and the `conquest-heroes-1v1` golden. The details are in [44 · Hero abilities](44-hero-abilities.md).
- **H3, heroes in Field Battles:** the army builder buys up to three heroes, with levels for points; a side's first hero takes the general's place (leadership, speech, the fall) instead of a soldier of the best regiment, and is its side's champion.

Since H2, the heroes whose strength is in their abilities (the Maestro, the Magnate) earn their place through them: the Maestro's Crescendo and Symphony, the Magnate's Compound Interest and Market Crash.

## Real use

Heroes are the smallest bodies on the field, so passages are never the problem; how they fight and travel with an army is.

- **Through gates, bridges, fords and walls:** a hero's footprint is 1.0 by 1.0 tiles (the Ronin, the Undertaker) to 1.4 by 2.0 (the mounted Daimyo), under every passage's width check (the clearance check covers it with the rest of the roster). Heroes don't man walls, so a hero goes through the gate.
- **On the march:** in a group order a hero is a member of the brigade like any regiment; as a block one model wide it packs into any row and never forms a column. Heroes walk at 3.25 to 5.5 (the Daimyo rides), against 2.25 to 7.0 for regiments, so most outrun the line they lead; the computer players move them in the army's brigade, not on their own.
- **In combat** (retuned 2026-11-22 for Mario: "strong shock impact, strategic assets, but not invincible; three regiments should kill one in about 30 s; it should be able to fight its way out"):
  - **Toughness:** hit points about 8% lower, melee defence 5 lower and growth a level about 10% lower than the first rosters.
  - **Exposed:** melee is limited by frontage, so only a few men of a line reach a one-man block. Melee blows on a hero land at 130% (`HERO_EXPOSED_PERCENT`), so a few regiments together do pull one down (they push it no harder).
  - **Shock:** every soldier a hero kills in melee costs the regiment it struck 4 morale (`HERO_SHOCK`), shown as "hero's onslaught" among the reasons it wavers. Above its breaking point a regiment can't rout, so the shock tells once it is bloodied: it breaks sooner.
  - **Breaking out:** a hero given a move order while it fights cuts its way out, as a fall back (each foe gets one parting blow and nobody can lock it in melee for up to 8 s), but from any side and at its marching pace, facing the way it goes. It keeps the way and pace of its move order. Once every enemy regiment's block is more than 4 tiles off (`CLEAR_TILES`), or at the end of the 8 s, it marches on as usual and can be caught again. A second move order while breaking out breaks out again. Attack-moves and the Fall back order don't break out; a hero falling back is caught at its flank or rear like anyone.
  - **Measured** in the `heroes` stress scenario (real data, Daimyo): beside his Yari he breaks a Skitterlings regiment head-on in 10 s. Two Chitin Guard and a Mantid Reapers regiment attacking a fresh level-1 Daimyo on open ground, four fights in four places: they take 91% of his hit points in their first 30 s and kill him in 31, 29 and 15 s; in the fourth they break first. Ordered out of the same ring after 3 s of fighting, he is clear in under 4 s, having lost about 60. Each fight's dead level a hero up, and a level-3 hero lasts about twice as long. The scenario checks the 70%-in-30-s mark (over the four fights) and the break-out on every pull request.
- **Leadership and generals:** in Conquest there are no generals, so heroes are the only leaders. In Field Battles (H3) a side's first hero replaces the general, so the two never stack.
- **Traffic at its building:** a revived or hired hero comes out at the building's front like a trained regiment and joins the rally point, so it queues with the rest.
- **Computer players** count a hero as a fighting regiment and march it with the army. Keeping it a row behind the front and pulling it back when badly hurt is a follow-up, as is counting its levels in the AI's fight estimates.
