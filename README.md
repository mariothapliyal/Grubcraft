# Grubcraft

A six-player LAN real-time strategy game where Warcraft III's base-building meets Shogun: Total War's regiment battles, fought over the corpse of a fallen god. Built on a lean custom engine for Mac and Windows, with a 6 GB memory budget for a full 6-player war.

- **Conquest** (main mode): gather Grub and Marrow, build a base, tech up, and command regiments with formations, flanks and morale. 2 to 6 players, 20 to 60 minutes.
- **Field Battle**: pre-built armies, one battlefield, 5 to 20 minutes.
- **Four factions** with four different economies: the Ember Clans, the Brood, the Choir and the Carrion Concord.

## Status

*Checked against the code on 2026-10-31.* Both modes are playable, against friends and against the computer.

- **Conquest** is the main mode: workers, buildings, three tiers, supply and upkeep, each faction's own economy (Clans Honor and Edicts, Brood larvae and root-veins, Choir chords and Resonance, Concord caravans), fog of war, and Annihilation and Titan's Heart victories. It plays on grown maps (Small, Medium or Large, up to six players) with rivers, fords, woods, villages and trails, or on Ribcage Pass and The Four Teeth.
- **Field Battle**: a points budget and army builder in the lobby, 90 seconds of deployment, then the battle; the side left holding the field wins.
- **Playing together:** up to six machines on one network, or over the internet with Tailscale, with computer players at four levels (Recruit, Veteran, General, Warlord) in any slot. Every match is saved as a replay.
- **Battles:** regiments with formations, frontage (only soldiers touching the enemy strike), duels between soldiers, charges, braced spears, morale that breaks a regiment only once it is cut down to its breaking point, veteran ranks, ambushes from woods and tall grass, a general who rides with his men in Field Battles, queued orders, attack-move and deep lines.
- **Fortifications:** walls drawn along curves for every faction with three levels, gates and gatehouses, manned walls and ladders, towers that fire (plus Bolt and Blast towers with level 2s), stakes, barricades, hidden traps, drawn bridges with upgrades, and the Walled Town bonus.
- **A living land:** weather (on by default), night, seasons in Conquest, mud, woods that burn (and lightning that lights them), clearing land to build, rivers that rise and flood their banks, flash floods, currents, and logs and rafts that drift, snag fords and jam bridges.
- **Not built yet:** heroes, Brood evolutions, reinforcing regiments, Titan Events, the Grubfolk market and mercenaries, the tutorial, the campaign, and the rest listed in [the quick reference, section 8](docs/quick-reference.md#8-built-vs-not-yet-built).

What's next is in the [restart plan](docs/29-restart-plan.md#progress) and the [roadmap](docs/13-roadmap.md). Start with the [design documents](docs/README.md); for "how do I do X in the game?", read the [player quick reference](docs/quick-reference.md).

## Building and running

You need [Rust](https://rustup.rs) (stable). Then, from this folder:

```sh
cargo run --release -p gc_game          # play the current build
cargo test --workspace                  # all tests, including determinism
cargo run --release -p gc_bench         # simulation benchmark with budget checks
cargo run -p gc_bake                    # check data/*.ron and bake target/grubcraft.pak
cargo run --release -p gc_game --features profile   # build with Tracy profiling
./scripts/bundle-macos.sh               # macOS: build Grubcraft.app (universal) and zip it
```

**Menus:** the game opens on the main menu: **Play**, **Sandbox**, **Replays**, **Unit viewer**, **Settings**, **Guide** and **Exit**. **Play** lets you host a lobby or join one. The host picks the mode (Conquest or Field Battle), the map (with **New map** for another and a map code to type in), its **Size**, the tempo, the victory conditions, the time of day, the weather and, in Conquest, the seasons; up to six players in six slots, with AI players at Recruit, Veteran, General or Warlord; each player picks a faction, a colour (Red, Violet, Cyan, Bone, Amber or Jade) and a team. Games hosted on your network show up in the Join list, or type the host's IP address (port 47820) or join code. The host's machine runs the AI. A **Conquest** match starts every side with its main building, workers and stores ([quick reference, Economy](docs/quick-reference.md#2-economy)). A **Field Battle** gives every side a points budget (2,000, 4,000 or 8,000) to buy regiments from its faction's roster in the lobby (**Pick for me** fills it), then 90 seconds to deploy them on its start; the first side left holding the field for ten seconds wins (the panel says VICTORY or DEFEAT). The factions are tuned to win about half their games against each other in both modes; the Brood against the Choir in Field Battles is the pairing furthest out (the Brood wins 25 to 35%). **Settings** holds your name, colour and faction, UI scale, graphics, audio and accessibility choices (saved to a `settings.ron` file in your user config folder; Clarity, Tilt-shift, Reduced screen shake, Full blood, the Graphics preset's particle density and the master and music volumes work, while V-sync, frame cap, the other volumes, the colour-blind palette, high-contrast banners and hints are saved but not acted on yet). **Unit viewer** shows each unit and hero of the four factions as a 3D model on a plinth, with an orbit camera, a button for each animation and Stats and Abilities tabs. The **Guide** (main menu, the top bar's **Guide** button, or **U**) has a Quick Start page and lists every building and troop of the four factions, with its role, stats and upgrades.

**Playing with friends (LAN):** everyone runs the same build (the lobby shows each player's build and won't start until they match). One player chooses Play, then Host. The others choose Play and pick the game from "Games on your network", or type the host's IP address and press Join. On a Mac, allow Grubcraft to find devices on your local network and to accept incoming connections when macOS asks. Each joiner ticks Ready, the host adds AI players if wanted and presses Start. If someone's connection drops during the battle, the game pauses for up to 90 seconds while they come back (the host can press P to carry on without them); set **On drop** to Play on in the lobby to keep playing instead. The lag between giving an order and seeing it adjusts itself to the slowest connection. If the machines ever disagree about the battle, each one saves a desync report in a `desync-reports` folder next to its settings file; send those to the developers. If a game doesn't show up in the list, type the join code the host's lobby shows (like `K7QD-M2XA`) or the host's address; that usually still works (some Wi-Fi networks block the broadcast that finds games). To play beyond one network, everyone installs Tailscale and the host shares their computer with the others; the host's lobby then shows a **Tailscale code** they join with ([11 · Networking](docs/11-networking.md#playing-beyond-one-wi-fi-network)). The host can set a password on the Play screen; friends type it in the Password box. Everything sent between the machines is encrypted. From the terminal, `--host` opens a lobby straight away and `--join ADDRESS` (or a join code) joins one; add `--password WORD` for a locked lobby.

**Controls:** you are red. Left-click a red regiment to select it, or left-drag a box around several (a green rubber-band rectangle shows the box; Shift adds or removes). Right-click the ground to march the selection there side by side, facing the way they travel; right-drag to draw the line they should form instead (its length sets how wide they stand, and they face away from you); hold Alt while you drag for a deep line: the drag is its front, each regiment keeps its frontage and those that don't fit stand behind (handy across a gate or bridge). A big selection forms several lines. Right-click an enemy regiment to attack it. Hold Shift while you right-click to queue a march or an attack after the regiment's current orders (up to four; any order without Shift replaces the queue). **A** (Advance) then a left-click marches the selection there, stopping to fight any enemy within 12 tiles on the way before marching on; **W** then a click on open ground does the same. Your back two rows are Yumi archers, who shoot at enemies in range on their own; blocks that touch fight, and flank, rear and charging hits hurt more. Regiments that lose heart waver (a blinking amber banner), then rout (a white banner): they break ranks and run, ignoring orders, and rally once the enemy is gone; a regiment that routs twice is shattered (a grey banner) for good. Regiments in woods (dark green, with trees) or tall grass (pale tufts) are hidden from enemies who aren't close: a moss-green flag above one of yours means the enemy can't see it, and enemies you can't see aren't drawn. Charging out of hiding into an enemy that hadn't seen you springs an **ambush** (it loses a lot of morale and fights badly for a few seconds; an "AMBUSH!" message and a drum and horn tell both sides), and so does an archers' first ordered volley from hiding; hidden archers only shoot when you order them to. Woods also slow a march (cavalry and monsters most), and their trees catch some of the arrows shot into or out of them. In the sandbox **Shift+V** shows every regiment, hidden or not. The top bar's **Place** button (or **B**) opens the **Place panel**: a cloth with two tabs, **Field types** (Yari Ashigaru, Skitterlings, Shardlings, Bone Pikes, Yumi Ashigaru, each with its soldiers, speed and points) and **All factions** (a chip for each of the four factions, then every fighting troop of its roster with soldiers, speed and Grub and Marrow cost; workers and the Caravan are left out), and a row of the four sides. Click a troop to choose it and start placing, click a side to place for it, then left-click the field; picking a troop from the other tab starts the sandbox again on that unit table, clearing the field; **[** and **]** still step through the types. **K** lets you try buildings in the sandbox: it switches the click to placing the chosen building for the side you command (**[** and **]** choose which, **R** or the wheel turns it; a see-through ghost shows whether it fits), **Delete** over a building takes it away, and right-clicking an enemy building with regiments selected sends them to knock it down. Damaged buildings show a bar over them. In Conquest your workers build them instead (below). Regiments find their own way around cliffs and deep water: a selected regiment shows its route as a dotted line, and **G** shows the ground no regiment can cross. Ctrl (Cmd on a Mac) + a number key saves the selection as a control group; the number recalls it (Shift adds it), and pressing it twice centres the camera on it. The bar along the bottom has a **minimap** (click or drag to move the camera, right-click to send the selection there) and an **army tray** with a card for each selected regiment (click to select it alone, Shift-click to toggle, double-click to centre the camera on it). The **command grid** at the bar's right end has **Q** Move, **W** Attack and **A** Advance (press, then left-click the ground or an enemy; Esc cancels), **E** Burn (set nearby woods alight), **R** Formation, **Z** Brace (spears and pikes only), **X** Torches (at night), **C** Plant stakes (Conquest) and **V** Fall back; **O** steps through the army shapes for group moves (Line, Deep, Hammer, Square, Plain and three of your own, saved with **Shift+O**). Hold, Skirmish, Run and the hero abilities are not built yet. When one of your regiments is losing men, is ambushed or breaks, and it is off screen, a badge on the screen edge points that way (a red `!`, an orange `A` or a pale `R`); click it or press **Space** to look there. The top bar names the mode, the side you command, the game speed and the clock, and has a **? Help** button; **H** or **F1** (or the button) opens the **Marshal's Scroll**, a list of every key in groups (Orders, View, Sandbox) with what the computer players are doing, and **Esc** or the same key closes it. In a team match the top bar also has a **Hand over** button: it gives your whole army to your first living ally, who then commands it (click again for the next ally, and once more to take it back; its label shows who has it). Hover a command button for a moment to see its full name, key and what it does. With regiments selected the tray shows a banner in your colour with the regiment's name, soldier count and health and morale bars (in Conquest **F1** still finds an idle worker while one stands idle). Heroes are not in the game yet, but **Shift+H** previews the hero panel with a made-up hero. Pan with the arrow keys, screen edges or a two-finger trackpad scroll; zoom with the mouse wheel or a pinch; rotate with Alt + middle-drag, a two-finger twist, or the `,` and `.` keys; Home or a middle-click resets the rotation. The camera tilts with the zoom: about 55° from the default zoom out, easing down to 30° at the closest zoom, so close up you look more along the ground and see the sky. **F9** steps through the lighting looks (Noon, Golden hour, Overcast, Storm, Dusk, Night; Golden hour is the default), **F12** steps through the weather to preview it (Clear, Rain, Snow, Fog, easing in over a few seconds; a match follows the lobby's **Weather**, Changing by default; rain wets the ground into puddles that dry slowly, splashes near the camera, and flashes with lightning, a bolt and a clap of thunder a moment later; wind leans it; a thin mist lies on rivers and pools, thickest at dawn and after rain, gone at noon; Clarity turns it off; snow settles on the ground, trees and soldiers and melts away slowly, rain darkens everything it soaks, and a clear night shows stars and cloud banks drift in the sky, seen when riding with a regiment and mirrored in water), `--weather NAME` opens every match in one (a lobby match follows its own weather and its clock, which moves the light from dawn through noon and dusk to night; `--battle LEVEL --time-of-day NAME --sky NAME` picks them from the command line), and `--look NAME` (for example `--look storm`) opens every match with one, in place of the lobby's **Time of day** (Golden hour, Noon, Dusk or Overcast, the host's pick). Each land brings its own air round the camera: an amber haze over the Waste, a green mist in the Fen, cold clear air on the Highland. **Clarity** in Settings turns off the vignette, film grain and heavy colour grading for competitive play. **Tilt-shift** in Settings (off by default) softens the top and bottom of the view like a table of miniatures; riding with a regiment (**Y**) shows sun shafts when the sun is in front (both are off with Clarity). **F10** opens the light tuning panel (Up and Down choose a value, Left and Right move it, L forces one land's air, Enter writes the values to the log), and `--air waste` (or any land) starts with one land's air everywhere. **F8** turns on the director camera, which finds the fight and frames it (moving the view takes it back; a replay you watch starts with it on, with letterbox bars and slow motion for the big moments), and `--watch FILE` opens a recorded battle at once. When a battle begins, letterbox bars slide in and out (Clarity turns them off), and charges that hit home, ambushes and falling buildings shake the screen (Settings, Reduced screen shake turns that off); the HUD stays still. At night a regiment that burns torches (X) lights the ground round it and carries flames, and Brood regiments glow faintly green. Marching soldiers kick up dust and arrows raise puffs where they land, and a charge that hits home rolls a cloud (the Graphics preset in Settings thins them: all on High, three quarters on Balanced, a third on Battery); `--fx-demo` sets off every effect (dust, smoke, fire, sparks, wounds, debris, ghost-light) in slow motion at the camera's focus, and `--fx-still` freezes them at several ages for a picture. With a regiment selected, **Y** puts the camera just above head height behind its rear ranks, looking where it faces, so you feel you are in the line (you can still select and give orders, and it works while you watch a replay); press **Y** or **Esc** again, or pan, zoom or rotate, to go back to the bird's-eye view. **`** (backquote) or **F11** shows the dev overlay with frame, tick and memory stats (and prints memory to the terminal); **Esc** returns to the map first while riding, otherwise opens the menu (Resume, Play, New sandbox, Settings, Exit). Start with `--overlay` to show it from the first frame, `--no-menu` to skip the main menu, or `--battle recruit|veteran|general|warlord` to go straight into a Field Battle against one computer player of that level. `--map ribcage_pass` or `--map four_teeth` (or `--map` with a map file's path) opens the sandbox on that map instead, to look round it. `--seed N` sets the map seed the Host a game card starts on, and the map a `--battle` is fought on.

**In Conquest:** select workers and press **F** or **B** for the build menu (hover a button for what the building is for), pick a building and click the ground; **D** lays a new main building. Right-click a seep or vein to gather, or your unfinished or damaged building to build or mend it; **S** and a drag clears woods, scrub or thicket first. Select a building to train from it (the grid shows what it trains and the price), right-click the ground for its rally point, and press **V** on your main building for the next tier (or on a gate for its gatehouse, or a tower for its level 2). Walls, bridges, field works and traps come from the build menu too: drag to draw a curve, click for corners, and **U** raises a finished wall or bridge a level. The faction power buttons under the top bar (**F2** to **F5**) spend Honor or Resonance. The [quick reference](docs/quick-reference.md) has every key, cost and rule.

**Computer players:** **I** hands the side you command to the computer (press again to step it up from Recruit to Veteran to General to Warlord, and once more to take the side back). The Marshal's Scroll (**H**, **F1** or the **? Help** button in the top bar) shows each computer player's personality, level, battle plan and what it expects ("Hold the line, advancing, expects a close fight"), and the latest thing it decided for the side you command. With Tab and I you can set two computer players against each other and watch.

To measure memory on your machine: `cargo run --release -p gc_game -- --frames 600` runs for 600 frames and prints the numbers. Measurement flags: `--latency 1|2|3` (frames in flight), `--render-scale 0.25..1` (render fewer pixels than the window has), `--draw all|terrain|blocks|none` (turn parts of the frame off), `--lod 0..3` (draw every soldier at one level of detail), `--distance 14..260` (start with the camera that far from the ground), `--showroom all|buildings|NAME[,NAME...]` (stand the named soldier models, as spelled in `assets-src/models.ron`, in a row on an empty field under a fixed camera, cycling through idle, walk, attack and death, to review them; `--showroom-clip idle|walk|attack|shoot|cheer|die|march|look|kill|finish|blows|reel` holds one clip instead, `march` walking with the attack on the upper body, `look` idling while looking round, `kill` playing a half of a matched kill over and over, `finish` a blow struck down at a man on the ground, `blows` a soldier's blows in a fight one after another and `reel` the ways the men of a regiment reel from a hit; it does not combine with `--sandbox`, `--battle`, `--host` or `--join`), `--viewer NAME|start` (opens the Unit viewer, over the main menu, on the named unit or the first with a model; not with `--sandbox`, `--battle`, `--conquest`, `--host`, `--join` or `--showroom`), and `--no-shadows`, `--no-glow` and `--no-aa` (turn off the sun's shadows, the glow around bright light and the anti-aliasing).

## Layout

| Path | What |
|---|---|
| `crates/gc_fixed` | Deterministic fixed-point maths (no floats) |
| `crates/gc_assets` | Asset pack format: writer and zero-copy reader |
| `crates/gc_data` | Game data tables (units, rosters, buildings, factions) as the game reads them |
| `crates/gc_sim` | The deterministic simulation; runs identically on every machine |
| `crates/gc_map` | Maps: the seeded and grown map makers, the RON map format and loader, and the terrain rules the game and `gc_mapcheck` share |
| `crates/gc_match` | A match without a window: setting out armies, deployment, headless play and the balance tables, shared by the game, tests, AI and benchmarks |
| `crates/gc_replay` | Replay format, recorder and the `gc_replay` runner (`verify`, `bless`, `info`, `compare`) for the golden replays |
| `crates/gc_audio` | Sound: ADPCM bank, 32-voice mixer, lock-free command queue, `cpal` output |
| `crates/gc_platform` | Memory accounting and OS process stats |
| `crates/gc_render` | wgpu renderer (Metal, DirectX 12, Vulkan) |
| `crates/gc_ui` | Menus, lobby, settings, the Guide and the battle HUD (immediate-mode UI, no GPU code) |
| `crates/gc_net` | LAN play: UDP transport, finding games on the network, join codes, the lobby protocol and host-sequenced lockstep turns |
| `crates/gc_ai` | Computer players for Field Battle and Conquest: battle tactics, difficulty levels, personalities and the Conquest minds; they read the sim and send ordinary commands |
| `crates/gc_game` | The game application |
| `tools/gc_bake` | Baker: compiles `data/*.ron` into the asset pack |
| `tools/gc_artbake` | Art baker: rigged glTF soldiers and buildings into `assets/models.pak` |
| `tools/gc_scenebake` | Scenery baker: trees, bushes, grass and rocks into `assets/scenery.pak` |
| `tools/gc_sfx` | Sound generator: synthesises the effects and music loops, and layers the battle cries from recorded voices, into `assets/audio/sounds.gcsnd` |
| `tools/gc_bench` | Headless benchmarks with budget gates |
| `tools/gc_mapcheck` | Map validator: `cargo run -p gc_mapcheck -- check` checks every map in `data/maps/` for validity and fairness; `png FILE OUT.png` draws one; `gen RECIPE SIZE SEED OUT.ron` writes a Field Battle or Conquest recipe map from a seed; `battle AREA ROUTE FEATURE fort\|open SEED OUT.ron` writes a campaign battle map |
| `tools/ground_textures` | Bakes the ground and scenery detail textures from ambientCG sets |
| `scripts/` | Mac and Windows bundling, the benchmark gate, the CI smoke run and texture generators |
| `assets/` | Baked packs (models, scenery, detail textures) and the sound bank |
| `assets-src/` | Art sources and their catalogue ([MANIFEST](assets-src/MANIFEST.md)) |
| `data/` | Game data in RON: `units.ron` (sandbox Field types), `rosters/` (every faction's units and buildings), `guide/` (the in-game Guide text), `ai/` (personalities and openings), `maps/` (Ribcage Pass and The Four Teeth) |
| `replays/` | The golden replays CI plays back on every platform ([README](replays/README.md)) |
| `packaging/` | Read-me files that go in the Mac and Windows zips |
| `docs/` | Design documents ([index](docs/README.md)) |
